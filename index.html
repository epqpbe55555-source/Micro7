Chlamydiae & Actinomycetes — Medical Microbiology, Lec. 7

Section 01

Overview

The rules, definitions and facts worth knowing cold — not the full lecture, just what carries the exam.

Chlamydia — core facts

  • Three human species: C. trachomatis, C. pneumoniae, C. psittaci.
  • Split by antigenic composition, inclusions, sulfonamide susceptibility, disease.
  • Genus-specific antigen = heat-stable LPS with KDO (2-keto-3-deoxyoctanoic acid).
  • Viewed as Gram-negative but cannot make their own ATP → obligate intracellular parasites.

EB vs RB

  • EB (Elementary Body): extracellular, infectious, metabolically inactive.
  • RB (Reticulate Body): intracellular, metabolically active, replicating — the "energy parasite."
  • RB makes its own nucleic acid, lipids, proteins — but not ATP.
  • Cycle: attach → uptake → EB→RB → binary fission/inclusion → RB→EB → release.

Staining

  • EB stains purple with Giemsa (host cytoplasm stains blue).
  • RB stains blue with Giemsa (larger, non-infective).
  • Gram reaction is negative/variable — not useful for ID.

C. trachomatis — ocular

  • Trachoma = chronic kerato-conjunctivitis, serovars A, B, C.
  • Incubation 3–10 days; lacrimation, mucopurulent discharge, conjunctival hyperemia.
  • Progresses to scarring, eyelid deformity, blindness with 2° bacterial infection.

C. trachomatis — genital

  • Men: non-gonococcal urethritis.
  • Women: urethritis, cervicitis, PID → sterility, ectopic pregnancy risk.
  • Neonate infected passing through birth canal — 20–60% of exposed infants infected.

Virulence factors (CT)

  • Tarp: actin rearrangement → endocytic uptake.
  • Inclusion-membrane proteins block lysosome fusion.
  • Secreted proteins interfere with NF-κB signaling (innate immunity).

Diagnosis & Rx (CT / CP)

  • Specimen must contain infected cells, not just secretions.
  • Best test: PCR (nucleic acid detection) > culture > serology.
  • Azithromycin — safe in pregnancy; doxycycline otherwise.

C. pneumoniae

  • Humans = only known host; often asymptomatic/mild.
  • Pharyngitis common; sinusitis/otitis media may accompany lower airway disease.
  • Rx: macrolides, tetracyclines, some fluoroquinolones.

C. psittaci — psittacosis

  • Acquired from birds; enters via respiratory tract.
  • Range: mild → severe pneumonia/sepsis; mortality up to 20% untreated (esp. elderly).
  • Culture is dangerous → prefer immunoassay/PCR; often treated on clinical grounds.
  • Rx: azithromycin, clarithromycin, erythromycin (± doxycycline in adults).

Actinomycetes

  • Anaerobic, Gram-positive, branching filaments (fungus-like) — mostly commensal/saprophyte.
  • Endogenous infection, e.g. after dental extraction → actinomycosis.
  • Classic sign: painless hard swelling with sulfur-granule abscess — "lumpy jaw" (cervicofacial form).
  • Key genera: Actinomyces, Nocardia, Actinomadura, Streptomyces.

Sterilization vs Disinfection

  • Sterilization: kills ALL microbial life, including spores.
  • Disinfection: kills pathogens, spores not guaranteed.
  • Disinfectant → non-living surfaces. Antiseptic → living tissue.
  • Physical: moist/dry heat, filtration, irradiation, sonic waves, pressure, sunlight.

Ideal disinfectant

  • Wide spectrum; works despite organic matter; active at any pH.
  • Non-toxic, non-corrosive, non-allergenic; stable on storage.
  • Fast-acting, high penetrating power, long shelf life.

Sterilization — Physical vs Chemical Methods

CLICK ANY METHOD TO EXPAND

Physical methods

Moist heat sterilization ▸
Below 100°C, at 100°C, and above 100°C (e.g. autoclaving) — most reliable for killing spores.
Dry heat sterilization ▸
Red heat, flaming, incineration, infrared radiation, hot air oven.
Filtration ▸
Filtration sterilization of liquids and of gases — removes rather than kills organisms.
Irradiation ▸
Ultraviolet (non-ionizing) radiation and ionizing radiation.
Other physical ▸
Sound (sonic) waves, pressure (pascalization), sunlight (solar disinfection).

Chemical methods

Chemisterilants ▸
Chemicals strong enough to sterilize completely, including spores.
Disinfectants ▸
Applied to surfaces of non-living objects to destroy microorganisms.
Antiseptics ▸
Safe on living tissue; prevent infection by inhibiting microbial growth.
Ideal properties ▸
Wide spectrum, active with organic matter present, active at any pH, non-toxic, stable, fast, high penetrating power.
